I did my work diligently today and gained something from it! We should believe that steady effort and accumulation, day after day, will eventually lead to achievements whose brilliance once seemed beyond reach!

I've noticed an aspect of my thinking that needs improvement. Because of the difficult life I once led between reality and theory, and amid collisions between different theories, I've developed a habit of thinking through multiple models. This is certainly an advantage when trying to fit an explanation to a real-world problem. But in the modern age, which demands deeper logical reasoning, it is far from enough. In fact, ever since childhood, what I called depth of thought has always depended on breadth and association. I've never used logic to build real depth. I should recognize this and cultivate the ability to think more deeply.

Today I noticed I've become particularly good at spelling words. A few little letters make a syllable; a few syllables make a unit of meaning. What an interesting thing! I can memorize three hundred new words and review three hundred old ones in a day and still want more. I've realized that with anything I do, once I have a little time to get used to it and discover that practice can itself be an enjoyable expression of my intellect, it stops being boring. Everything begins to flow.

How we view a challenge matters too. In middle school, I always thought the material was simple and that I ought to score the full 480 points. In practice, I came close. But in high school, I lacked confidence, and people around me were always saying one thing or another. I came to think that seventeenth in the year was an impressive result and spent all three years working toward that unremarkable rank, never going further. Yesterday and today's vocabulary practice are an example: once I have the method, I can move quickly. I don't need to compare myself with anyone in a group or online. I am unique too. If I believe I can, I can create achievements more brilliant than those of anyone I have known. Sun Haiming once told me that high-school material is limited and repetitive. Once I accepted that view, things really did go much more smoothly.

The limits of your world are the limits of what you believe.

Believe in the power of belief! Believe in the power of accumulation!
