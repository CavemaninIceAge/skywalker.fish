As I walked out of the library tonight, having just finished last year's Mathematics II transfer-exam paper well above the cutoff, I suddenly realized this was my twenty-fifth timed practice round for the computer-based exam. Beyond those, the algorithm textbooks I've read and the focused problem sets I've worked through are too numerous and varied to count.

I drifted through Beijing's summer night, humid and hot, clear and vast, like Huangshan in Anhui on a bright night. For a moment, the air felt like the days just before and after my gaokao. Life has so many moments like this, suspended and unresolved. We may be afraid, we may be impatient, but ‘that thing’ approaches at a steady pace, examines our preparations, and in the space of a few days delivers a verdict that turns the course of history.

I never imagined there would be a day like this. Nor did I imagine that, when it came, I would think this way.

I've imagined expressing joy with composure, imagined existential accusations against the world's injustice or praise for its grace. Instead, I simply cycled to the astronomy department and felt a complicated, hard-to-express satisfaction, with only one clear thought: this is a feeling I may rarely have again in my life. I need to learn to enjoy it. Remember this joy, this steadiness, this faint unease.

Even enjoy the possibility of failure. Looking at an empty VS Code window, I still occasionally make a catastrophic mistake and spend tens of minutes finding it. When you go out and do things, there will be wins and losses. In the end, I hope to be someone who can bear to lose.

The chance of being struck by lightning in a lifetime is small. But if you never chase the storm, you will never catch that bolt.

What is past cannot be recalled. The road ahead is bright.
