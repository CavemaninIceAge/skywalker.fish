Goebbels’s early life seems so much like mine. Having once been weak, he admired power and tried to become powerful. Being diligent and outstanding, he discovered that an unjust fate could be changed, that a person need not remain one among the masses. Goebbels liked to dream of writing, and his prose grew ever more accomplished. He once immersed himself in an imaginary world. One day he drew Germany into his fevered imagination too. I cannot help imagining what I might say in different circumstances.

On my birthday in October

I have settled in now, for a full week.

People say that moving between different environments over a long period can encourage the growth of new neurons, improving responsiveness, perception, and memory. The people I have particularly liked throughout my life have had such experiences too: Fang Yuxuan moving from Daqing to Harbin, Wang Tongxu from Zhaodong to Harbin, Wu Zonghan from Jixi to Harbin, Zhang Jingbo from Daqing to Beijing and then from Beijing to Washington, D.C. There is hardly any need to mention examples from antiquity. They always possess a vitality beyond the ordinary, and a slightly rebellious will to power within the rules.

Now I have become one of them. More and more people say I have presence, that I am determined and hardworking, that I excel in many fields. Most people seem to feel refreshed in my company. I can feel it too: I can achieve every goal I set for myself. I have ideas and am willing to work for them. I have talent, and that talent can bring me a future without limit. I have perceptiveness. I can clearly sense different people’s opinions of me, plan my response, and give my words authority within a small group.

From birth to now, I have always been drifting. At present, I am at Deqiang High School in Harbin.

At first it was passive drifting: between Daqing, Harbin, and Sanya; passed endlessly between my parents and grandparents; traveling through all sorts of places in China. Later it was wandering by choice: through affection, through humanistic feeling, through setbacks and the hurt of entanglement; through striving, saying goodbye to people from the past, becoming absorbed in real life, accepting challenges, longing for freedom and a perfect life, walking bravely toward tomorrow, losing myself in another relationship, waking through failure, living in Beijing, living at university. At last I have arrived at today.

This year I am nineteen, and still in high school. Should that make me sad?

Fortunately, you are all still here. Fortunately, the future is still here too.

And I am still young, still full of life. My gaokao result still leaves me desolate. I have to admit that some failures are absolute failures. There is nothing in them worth treating as a virtue. What has real value is the effort a person makes to face a crisis. And in Harbin’s ever-cooling autumn, I can still see a whole sky of stars above Deqiang.

These stars are different from those I saw at the high school affiliated with Harbin Institute of Technology, beside No. 3 High School, and in so many other places. Yet they still shine on my life.

At nineteen, I did not see the peach blossoms by Weiming Lake. There are still stars in the sky.

Should I be glad that I did not choose the earth, but chose the sky again?

Every passing moment will pass. And what has passed will become a cherished memory.
