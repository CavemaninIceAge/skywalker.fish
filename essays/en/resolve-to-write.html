—Where did we come from? Where did I come from?

Early autumn suddenly descended on Beijing, close behind an autumn rain. Last night I ate on Qianmen Street with a group of relatives I had just met. Because of a stomachache, I did not go with them to Tiananmen afterward and found myself alone on Beijing’s crowded subway. Passageways always have the strongest sense of perspective. In the depth of the subway carriage, the thin mist between me and the world was broken apart. Every current of air, every beam of light, every detail of every pore on a person’s body became concrete and perceptible. Suddenly I realized that somehow I had appeared among the crowds of Beijing; that I had grown very tall and no longer felt the clothes on my body within the narrow awareness of my middle-school years. Had I suddenly become a university student? Had I entered an entirely unfamiliar world?

It felt as if I had been thrown straight from middle school into today, as if growing up had taken only an instant. From childhood to adulthood, how had all of this happened? Where, exactly, had I come from?

So I plan to write down this series of my stories, connecting the end of childhood to the beginning of youth. Too much has happened along the way. Great enthusiasm and great sorrow have alternated, and many weighty events are still difficult to make sense of. I hope this writing will give me an account I can live with, and help me see where I truly want to go next.

“The present and the future are both extensions of the past. The causes of history forge the results of the present. Everything in our reality—achievement or setback, victory or difficulty—has its beginnings in the past.”

“If we forget the past, we cannot face the present and the future correctly.”
