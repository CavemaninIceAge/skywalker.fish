Two things happened yesterday. First, Z and I spoke on the phone for forty minutes and restored friendly relations. Second, my grandmother returned to Harbin. I do not know when she will leave. During the night she went from room to room, over and over, waking me three or five times. I was still tired when I got up this morning.

At some point, both were people I trusted to help rebuild my life, only for them to hurt me more deeply instead. The logic of their hurt was like Qu Daying’s. Both acted in the name of family and friendship, “thinking of me” as they pressured me to do things I disliked. They were patient and stubborn, impossible to resist, often stirring up the torment of a nightmare I could not escape. At that time, before I had found my own resolve, I kept trying to communicate with them. They kept “caring about me,” tormenting me and repeatedly making me do things I disliked. All I could do was wonder whether I was the one in the wrong.

I think of the countless times Z deceived and pressured me, the many times my grandmother spoke wildly disconnected things like someone mentally unwell, while I kept trying to communicate and agree. I even sought her support for changing my subject choices. Had I forgotten that only a month earlier she had secretly found a ritual master to exorcise me?

For fuck’s sake, can these ghosts and monsters get out of my life?

I am beginning to understand the glorious success of New China’s expulsion of foreign aggression. Aggression does not merely destroy the material foundation on which we stand. It humiliates us in spirit, even shakes our confidence in ourselves. In modern history, for example, our nation not only had to endure the constraints of the unequal-treaty system and treatment as second-class citizens. More humiliating still, we had to examine the dregs of our traditional culture, genuinely believe that we were nationally inferior, and always have people bowing obediently before barbarous invaders in the name of “moving toward civilization.”

But today is different. “A nation has stood up.”
