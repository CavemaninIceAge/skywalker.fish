What will I remember high school by?

—A few medical records, a box of medicine, a spatula, a kite, an instant-noodle wrapper.

—Who knows how many incense tickets from weekends spent listening to the wind chimes at Jile Temple; an alcohol lamp and a large beaker for warming milk.

—A health code, printer cartridges, sacred-music scores, guitar books, a long roll of colored paper strips for folding lucky stars.

—Two trays of dried-up gouache, a harmonica, all sorts of strange little stuffed toys picked up from friends' homes, a collection of Nanjing, Changfeng, Chunghwa, and Zhongnanhai cigarette packets…

—Or the key that can no longer open the door of Room 303.

But ‘life’ here is a very narrow word. It really means only the living itself. In my first year of high school, my parents still stayed with me on weekdays. In my second year, I suddenly found myself entirely on my own. Much of the time, the world was too quiet around me, so I did everything I could to give my life more weight—walking by the river or around Jile Temple on weekends, wandering the island in the river, coming home at night to warm some milk for myself… Life sometimes settled into its own patterns. Even today, going swimming, I still instinctively buy a bottle of Mizone. Passing Tongjiang Street, I still look to see whether the old synagogue has a concert. Beef noodles always have to come with sugar-free soda. I still like eating fruit on a park bench…

Living happily is so important. If I had to rank ‘life, freedom, love, and living,’ I might say ‘living—freedom—life—love.’ Perhaps love comes last only because I haven't yet felt how precious it is.
