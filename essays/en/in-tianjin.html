After endless confusion and difficulty, trees and roads repeated themselves from dawn to dusk, along with the natural cycle of morning and evening. Unexpectedly, I managed to push through to Jizhou District in Tianjin. Just after passing the city’s vehicle checkpoint, I stopped on the right side of the road and ate an extraordinarily good bowl of noodles.

My aunt and I were arranging when she would pick me up in Tongzhou tomorrow. We settled on a time and place, rather vaguely. In my most ideal imagining, I would enter Beijing early at dawn and watch the round body of light rise over the land of Hebei. The Beijing boundary, Hebei’s vast earth, and the brand-new sun should coexist completely and perfectly in a single photograph. In words, that scene would be: “Morning light is born above the white tower and will one day illuminate the shadowed land.” It would also be an entirely new metaphor: I am already standing at the finish, and sunlight will illuminate every road I have traveled.

At one in the morning I was still awake, chatting with Gao Siyu about all the meals we would have when I got back. Remember to get up at seven tomorrow. I have already flown across thirteen hundred kilometers, with only the final sixty left before I break the tape. I have given everything, yet I still feel I have endless strength. Towns run together, beginning in Jizhou and seemingly stretching all the way to the very center of Beijing. A vast new metropolitan area with a radius of more than 120 kilometers is unfolding rapidly before me.

“Look forward to victory, to the traces of having existed. Long for the dawn, and forget all other voices.”
