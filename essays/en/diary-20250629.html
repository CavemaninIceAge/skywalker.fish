One: recognize and overcome the ‘midway plateau.’ I've been learning guitar and singing lately. My problem with guitar is that I can't change chords quickly enough. No matter how I practice, it doesn't improve; I've been stuck for a week. In singing, the theory of resonance is simple, but consistently making every note resonate in the intended cavity is so difficult. I haven't improved there in a week either. These obstacles don't sound complicated, but breaking through them requires an enormous amount of repetition. The encouraging thing is that they aren't just ordinary hurdles halfway along. They are the core of their fields. Once I overcome them, my understanding of guitar and singing will reach a professional level.

Tomorrow I'll try putting other things aside and practicing chord changes and vocal resonance over and over, working toward muscle memory. I hope I can get through these midway hurdles!

This isn't limited to guitar or singing; it's a general pattern in how things develop. Here is how I define the midway plateau: while learning a field, you meet one of its central difficulties. It doesn't sound complicated, but mastering it requires immense practice to form muscle memory. Once you get through it, your understanding of the field takes a huge leap. Take high-school English: starting from 130 points, improving further means an astronomical amount of repeated practice before you can consistently lose fewer than five points on the earlier sections. Reading comprehension really comes down to understanding the precise correspondence between what the passage refers to and what the question refers to; then you won't choose the wrong answer. But getting there takes an astronomical amount of practice in which you see no improvement. Similarly, learning conic sections takes three or four months of accumulation: every new problem stumps you, and you can barely apply what you just learned. Only then can you reach the advanced stage of knowing how to solve a conic-section problem as soon as you see it.

I often lose momentum midway through things, and showed similar patterns during my year retaking the gaokao. I've improved compared with before, but I'm still far from having fully overcome the midway plateau. To get through it, first recognize it clearly and understand both its difficulty and its significance. Then work diligently through to the end, keeping at it. Finally, persist in practice until you break through the obstacle.
