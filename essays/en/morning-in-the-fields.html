Sometimes I think: a new day arriving over the fields must be an astonishing sight.

But there are no fields at home.

Every morning, countless different forms of life are stirring. Even the same beam of scattered light creates an entirely different scene in a county town, in a city, over a field, on the sea, on a mountaintop, among the trees. Each is nurturing its own particular vitality and beauty.

With my imperfect color vision hidden behind blue-light-filtering lenses, has the world I see become a little duller still?

The morning light greets me with the freshest, most mysterious blue. I take off my glasses to look at it, imagining the countless human, animal, and plant lives waiting to awaken, while field mice and owls sink into sleep. Suddenly I love the whole world so much. I want to know every fresh, surging form of life. I imagine the countless things I could do in the day ahead, all the possibilities it might hold. I'm grateful for moments like this, which tell me I am not too old yet.
