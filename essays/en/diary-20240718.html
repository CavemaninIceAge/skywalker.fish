At seven on the morning of July 18, Dad and I got up and headed into the mountains. Yandang Mountain had very few visitors. Someone at the entrance was selling shuttle tickets between the attractions. I scoffed at the idea, intending to walk between them all. Once we entered, however, I realized I had completely misunderstood the area. There was no main admission gate or overall ticket, but five core attractions stood on either side of the main road, with long distances between them. After stubbornly walking through the first attraction, we finally bought shuttle tickets.

Yandang Mountain is a World Geopark and a national forest park. It lacks the celebrated labels of World Natural Heritage or Chinese National Geography, which suggests that its importance may be geological more than aesthetic, and that did seem true. Wenzhou in Zhejiang Province has put up many signs explaining how the rocks formed. Geological movements are always vast and astonishing. We walked sixteen kilometers of mountain paths that day. Because of the weather, we did not climb Yungang Peak. Dad probably felt the natural scenery was repetitive, so he suggested we skip similar attractions that afternoon and head to the foot of Huangshan instead.

Hangzhou is the railway hub of southeastern China. Surprisingly, there were almost no connecting routes from economically developed Wenzhou to Huangshan through anywhere else. Because we had not researched enough beforehand, we missed the 14:52 train. We took a taxi to Taizhou, a train to Hangzhou, then a taxi from Hangzhou South to Hangzhou West, passing beautiful West Lake, and just managed the connection to Huangshan North. Our lodging looked very good, but it was far from the route we needed for the next day’s climb.

We ate fermented mandarin fish and other dishes at a hotel called Dongpo-something. The food was excellent.

The next day we climbed. The ascent was long, but part of the pleasure was seeing the mountains from different angles. I have to say Huangshan is extraordinarily beautiful. Strange rocks were everywhere, the paths steep. We climbed at least a thousand vertical meters and walked sixteen kilometers that day. The upper reaches were magnificent. Halfway up we could already see a sea of mountains, but discovered that the higher we climbed, the better the views became. There is a lesson in that: do not become complacent or settle where you are.

It is the pleasure of testing your limits.

Today we wanted to go back up to see the remaining rock formations, but an unlicensed guide talked us into a scam attraction and a rafting trip. The raft spun round and round. I felt sick.

I rode a Fuxing train, and I like it. We passed Poyang Lake, which was truly magnificent. Today I realized:

1. Always plan in advance, in great detail, paying attention to every practical step. Break it down to each action. You need to know what you are doing.

2. Every judgment, good or bad, must stay faithful to the facts. For instance, I kept thinking we could climb Yandang Mountain at night, even though every source said it was only possible during the day. Because I fantasized that things should not be too bad, I kept imagining that perhaps something would change when we arrived, and planned around that imagined turn of events.

3. Do not let other people push you into a decision. Pause for about ten seconds before acting; in terms of the overall expected outcome, this is definitely an advantage. I made two mistakes that day. First, we got off the shuttle to Yandang Mountain railway station instead of staying on it, missed the earlier train, and ended up spending around 280 yuan in taxi fares. If I had not rushed to book the hotel at the foot of Huangshan, we could have saved another 170 yuan in transport costs.

4. After completing each stage of a task, spend another ten seconds or so checking it. Today, while editing an image for He Guangyi, this review caught that “0” should have been “0.0.”

5. Work efficiently, and give realistic deadlines so people can rely on you. Today, for example, I told He Guangyi I would finish the image by 20:15, then got on with it and finished. That was right.

6. Do not overcomplicate problems; break them down to practical methods. Use several broad perspectives to model reality. For example, I have recently learned to look at things from the perspective of early humans: men enjoying ball games as a simulation of hunting, unhappy people needing to go out as a simulation of moving camp, men competing because they want to lead the tribe.

7. Never spend money that should not be spent. But do not make a decision just to avoid a twenty-percent premium, or you will seriously reduce efficiency and it will not be worthwhile. Spend where you should and save where you can!
