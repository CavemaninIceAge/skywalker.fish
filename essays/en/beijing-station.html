Walking out of Beijing Railway Station, memories of countless visits across countless years overlap in the familiar warm wind blowing into my face. This place is like my own front door. I have come triumphant, come defeated, come back to begin again, come full of expectation, come and gone quietly. Those tall, thick-walled buildings always stand at the end of the upward passage. The station is always taking in and sending out people from everywhere, each carrying an ambition of their own. This metropolis is eternal. Only the people rise and fall.

No one is like Beijing. Since the ancestors of the Western Zhou arrived with their livestock and children to build a city here, Beijing has existed for more than two thousand years. From the fourteenth century to now, it has enjoyed a seven-hundred-year golden age. No one has a golden age that long, not even within a human lifetime of seventy years.

No one can buy Beijing. No one can possess all of Beijing for a lifetime. From the emperor to the common people, we rise and fall, living within Beijing.

We rise and fall. We live in Beijing.
