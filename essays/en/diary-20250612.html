Today brings the familiar subjects of time and hope. Time is the most precious thing in life. So is hope.

I woke naturally at 6:23 this morning, then somehow spent an hour on my phone and did not properly wake up until nearly eight. But even the time within a single day is so limited. This waste comes from habit, and I must replace it with a more valuable habit. Sun Haiming’s example makes this clear: even writing a little every day can, through steady accumulation, create an enormous and extensive body of text, while improving one’s abilities across disciplines.

So this morning I got up, ate, and cycled to the swimming pool at the University of Commerce. The water there is excellent; it is closer to home and saves time. After an hour’s swim I came out, thinking I would deliver two food orders before going home to sleep and eat, then prepare for my afternoon guitar lesson. But time will not stand still, and once something begins it takes on a momentum of its own. In the end I delivered four orders. Wind rushed wildly past my ears until I could barely hear the navigation, and when I checked what I had earned: twenty-three yuan. I know I cannot make everything pay for itself this way. I know that all the time heaven gives us is brief and limited. We must live fully, and our definition of living fully should be to develop our gifts and sensibilities. That requires us to learn more new things, fully and seriously, every day.

We must gain something from learning. In days when no authority forces us, we must study and record enough to avoid being filtered into mediocrity by society. So I want to find a notebook and keep writing down what I have learned each day, sorting out the ill-formed ideas in my mind. Gain something daily; small steps lead to success. For example, I had an hour’s guitar lesson this afternoon. I do not yet fully understand the relationship between fingers, string numbers, frets, strings, and how they feel under my fingers. I need to work thoroughly on this difficulty on my own if I am to succeed.

Progress in life consists of changing one small difficulty after another. This is the most important thing my year of repeating school taught me: people can improve, but improvement requires awareness and targeted practice. The process is bound to be slow, yet success through small steps is also something you can be certain of.

This afternoon I spent half an hour on my phone and then took another nap. I really should not do that! When I woke, there were still five hours left in the day, and I could not help asking myself:

What duties have you fulfilled toward tomorrow?

Reading Flowers for Algernon, I suddenly realized I had somewhat lost the ability to read long texts. But after an hour, I was getting better at it and my thinking was becoming clearer. This suggested a new model of self-improvement:

Rate of progress — long-term accumulation — visible results

The rate of progress depends on how disciplined one’s life is and how calm one’s thinking is; long-term accumulation depends on how much time one spends improving each day; visible results show how much one will achieve.

I should pay attention to the rate of progress too, and work toward a life of greater progress and more careful thought.
